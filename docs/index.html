Forty years later

Enhanced graphics

Blob, every nasty and all 512 screens, redrawn by hand with backdrops behind them. Hit F8 mid-game to flip straight back to 1985 and see how far he’s come.

Orchestral score

Strings and brass where the Spectrum managed a beep — on the menus, on the computer report, and on the big finish. Golp, he’s gone symphonic.

A telly from 1985

F9 steps through scanlines, a phosphor glow and the full bowed tube. Three flavours of nostalgia, or none at all — it’s your call.

PAL and NTSC modes

F11 swaps 50Hz, the Spectrum original, for 60Hz. For silky-smooth action on modern displays and a 20% speed boost.

Joypad welcome

D-pad or thumbstick, A to fire. Or cursor keys. Or Q A O P, if your muscle memory never really left the eighties.

No faffing about

macOS, Windows and Linux, all AOT — native code, just like C++ and Z80 assembly. No .NET runtime. Download, run, crash-land.

How to play

Flightboard computer

Nine pieces of core are scattered across the planet and the core wants all of them. Find them, carry them home, try not to get eaten on the way. That’s the whole job — the hard part is the 512 rooms in between.

Getting about

  • ↑ ↓ ← → Move. Left Alt fires the laser. That’s the default and you need do nothing.
  • Pad D-pad or left stick, A to fire. Plug it in before you start and the options screen will spot it.
  • Q A O P The original Spectrum layout, Space to fire. Pick it on the options screen — or define all seven keys yourself.
  • P Pause. Q quits while you’re paused, Esc quits from anywhere. On the Spectrum layout those two are S and D.

The function keys

F5
Snaps a screenshot
F8
Redrawn artwork ↔ the 1985 original
F9
CRT: off → scanlines → phosphor → full tube
F10
Backdrops on or off, out on the planet
F11
50Hz ↔ 60Hz
F12
Fullscreen

Whatever you flip is remembered next time — Blob isn’t going to ask twice. On a Mac, hold fn unless you’ve told it function keys are function keys.
